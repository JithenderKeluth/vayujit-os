## 1. Executive Summary

VAYUJIT OS is a local-first, AI-powered business operating system designed for solo entrepreneurs and small businesses that operate across e-commerce, content creation, digital marketing, automation, and analytics.

The platform will centralize business operations that are currently spread across multiple tools. It will help the user manage brands, products, marketplaces, media channels, workflows, AI agents, publishing, analytics, and approvals from one application.

The first release will focus on establishing a secure and extensible platform foundation, enabling basic commerce and media workflows, and proving that external integrations can be added without tightly coupling the entire system to a specific marketplace, social platform, or AI provider.

---

## 2. Problem Statement

Solo entrepreneurs and small businesses commonly rely on disconnected tools for:

- Product and inventory management
- Marketplace listing creation
- Content ideation and research
- Script and media generation
- Social publishing
- Workflow automation
- Revenue and performance analytics
- AI-assisted business decisions
- Access credential management
- Business documentation

This creates several problems:

1. Business data is fragmented.
2. The same information is entered repeatedly.
3. Brand consistency is difficult to maintain.
4. Content and commerce workflows are disconnected.
5. Marketplace and social data cannot be viewed together.
6. AI outputs are not governed by consistent approval and quality rules.
7. The user spends excessive time coordinating tools instead of growing the business.
8. Sensitive credentials are often stored unsafely.
9. Automation failures are difficult to detect and recover from.
10. Existing platforms usually focus on one domain rather than the entire operating model.

---

## 3. Product Vision

VAYUJIT OS will enable one person to operate like a coordinated business team by combining commerce, media, marketing, AI, workflows, and analytics in a single local-first application.

The platform will assist the user, not replace the user. High-impact actions will remain reviewable and auditable.

## 4. Product Goals

### 4.1 Business Goals

- Reduce time spent on repetitive business operations.
- Improve product and content consistency across channels.
- Enable reuse of product data for content generation.
- Centralize operational visibility.
- Support multiple brands from one application.
- Improve decision-making using unified analytics and recommendations.
- Create a foundation that can eventually support commercial distribution.

### 4.2 User Goals

- Manage brands, products, content, workflows, and integrations from one dashboard.
- Use AI without being locked into one provider.
- Review important AI-generated outputs before execution.
- Keep core business data local.
- Add new marketplaces and platforms without major rework.
- Understand workflow failures and recover safely.
- Track costs and value generated by AI-assisted workflows.

### 4.3 Technical Goals

- Use a modular monolith with strong domain boundaries.
- Keep integrations behind stable connector interfaces.
- Support both local and cloud AI providers.
- Secure credentials using encrypted local storage.
- Maintain auditable execution history.
- Support automated testing and architecture documentation.
- Enable future migration to distributed services only when justified.

## 9. Functional Requirements

### FR-001 Authentication

The system shall require users to authenticate before accessing protected functionality.

### FR-002 Local User Account

The system shall support creation and management of a local user account.

### FR-003 Secure Session

The system shall maintain a secure local session with configurable expiry.

### FR-004 Roles and Permissions

The system shall support roles and permissions for protected operations.

### FR-005 Brand Creation

The user shall be able to create, update, view, archive, and restore brands.

### FR-006 Brand Guidelines

The user shall be able to store tone, audience, language, visual, and messaging guidelines per brand.

### FR-007 Product Creation

The user shall be able to create, update, view, archive, and restore products.

### FR-008 Product Variants

The system shall support product variants with distinct SKUs, attributes, inventory, and pricing.

### FR-009 Product Assets

The system shall support local storage and management of product images, documents, audio, and video.

### FR-010 Inventory Tracking

The user shall be able to record and update product inventory.

### FR-011 Supplier Management

The user shall be able to manage supplier information and associate suppliers with products.

### FR-012 Marketplace Accounts

The user shall be able to register marketplace connector accounts without exposing credentials in the UI after storage.

### FR-013 Connector Capability Detection

The system shall identify which capabilities a connector supports.

### FR-014 Listing Draft Generation

The user shall be able to generate marketplace-specific listing drafts from product data.

### FR-015 Listing Approval

The system shall require approval before publishing or updating a listing unless the user explicitly configures a lower-risk automated rule.

### FR-016 Listing Synchronization

The system shall record synchronization state and errors for connected marketplaces.

### FR-017 Content Project Creation

The user shall be able to create content projects associated with a brand, product, campaign, or topic.

### FR-018 AI Idea Generation

The system shall generate content ideas using configured AI providers.

### FR-019 Script Generation

The system shall generate scripts using brand, product, audience, and channel context.

### FR-020 Structured AI Output

The system shall validate structured AI outputs against defined schemas.

### FR-021 AI Provider Selection

The user shall be able to choose a provider and model or allow configured routing.

### FR-022 Local AI Provider

The system shall support at least one local AI provider through an adapter.

### FR-023 AI Cost Tracking

The system shall record estimated or reported AI usage and cost.

### FR-024 Prompt Versioning

The system shall retain prompt versions and their execution history.

### FR-025 Agent Registry

The system shall support registration of agents with explicit tools, inputs, outputs, permissions, and approval requirements.

### FR-026 Workflow Creation

The user shall be able to create or import workflow definitions.

### FR-027 Workflow Execution

The system shall execute workflows and persist execution state.

### FR-028 Workflow Approval

The system shall pause workflows at configured approval steps.

### FR-029 Workflow Retry

The user shall be able to retry failed workflow steps.

### FR-030 Workflow Cancellation

The user shall be able to cancel eligible workflow executions.

### FR-031 Workflow Audit

The system shall record workflow step inputs, outputs, timing, status, and errors, subject to secret-redaction rules.

### FR-032 Publishing Approval

The system shall require user approval before external publishing in the MVP.

### FR-033 Publishing Result

The system shall record publishing success, external identifiers, URLs, and errors.

### FR-034 Notifications

The system shall notify the user about approvals, failures, connector problems, and completed workflows.

### FR-035 Dashboard

The system shall provide a dashboard showing business, workflow, connector, AI, and content status.

### FR-036 Audit Logging

The system shall record security-sensitive and business-critical actions.

### FR-037 Secret Storage

The system shall encrypt integration credentials and secrets at rest.

### FR-038 Secret Redaction

The system shall prevent secrets from appearing in logs, exports, and normal UI views.

### FR-039 Backup

The user shall be able to create a local backup of application data and metadata.

### FR-040 Restore

The user shall be able to restore from a valid backup after confirmation.

### FR-041 Import and Export

The system shall support structured import and export for selected business data.

### FR-042 Search

The system shall support search across brands, products, content, workflows, and activity.

### FR-043 Filtering and Sorting

The system shall support filtering and sorting on major list views.

### FR-044 Activity History

The system shall show relevant changes and execution history for major entities.

### FR-045 Connector Health

The system shall show connector authentication, availability, and synchronization status.

### FR-046 AI Evaluation

The system shall support configurable checks for output quality, safety, completeness, and structure.

### FR-047 Human Feedback

The user shall be able to rate, approve, reject, and comment on AI-generated outputs.

### FR-048 Analytics Export

The user shall be able to export selected analytics and reports.

### FR-049 Application Health

The system shall display backend, database, Redis, storage, AI provider, and connector health.

### FR-050 Safe Deletion

The system shall require confirmation for destructive actions and support soft deletion where appropriate.

## 10. Non-Functional Requirements

### NFR-001 Local Operation

Core application features shall remain usable locally without continuous internet connectivity.

### NFR-002 Windows Support

The MVP shall support a documented Windows installation and runtime process.

### NFR-003 Performance

Common local UI actions should provide feedback within 500 milliseconds when no external service is involved.

### NFR-004 API Responsiveness

Typical local API requests should complete within 1 second under normal development-scale usage, excluding AI and external integrations.

### NFR-005 Reliability

Workflow state shall survive application restarts.

### NFR-006 Recoverability

Failed workflows shall provide actionable error details and safe retry options.

### NFR-007 Data Integrity

Database writes shall use transactions where required to prevent partial business state.

### NFR-008 Security

The system shall follow least privilege and secure defaults.

### NFR-009 Encryption

Sensitive credentials shall be encrypted at rest.

### NFR-010 Auditability

Critical actions shall be attributable to a user or system process.

### NFR-011 Maintainability

Domain modules shall expose documented public interfaces and avoid direct access to another module’s internal storage.

### NFR-012 Testability

Core business logic shall be testable without launching the desktop shell.

### NFR-013 Accessibility

The desktop UI shall target WCAG 2.2 AA practices where applicable.

### NFR-014 Observability

The system shall provide structured logs, health checks, workflow history, and error correlation identifiers.

### NFR-015 Portability

The architecture shall avoid unnecessary Windows-only coupling in the backend and domain layers.

### NFR-016 Extensibility

New AI providers and connectors shall be addable through documented interfaces.

### NFR-017 Privacy

User business data shall not be sent to external services unless required by a user-configured operation.

### NFR-018 Secret Safety

Secrets shall never be committed to Git or included in diagnostic exports.

### NFR-019 Backup Validation

Backups shall include metadata required to verify compatibility and integrity.

### NFR-020 Versioning

Prompts, workflows, connectors, schemas, and APIs shall use explicit versioning.

### NFR-021 Idempotency

External side-effect operations shall use idempotency controls where supported.

### NFR-022 Cost Control

AI workflows shall support configurable cost limits and warnings.

### NFR-023 Dependency Security

Dependencies shall be scanned for known vulnerabilities.

### NFR-024 Documentation

Major modules shall include architecture, setup, operation, and troubleshooting documentation.

### NFR-025 Scalability

The MVP shall support the operational needs of one user managing multiple brands without requiring distributed infrastructure.

## 11. User Journeys

### 11.1 First-Time Setup

1. User installs and launches VAYUJIT OS.
2. System validates local dependencies.
3. User creates an owner account.
4. User configures backup location.
5. User optionally configures an AI provider.
6. User creates the first brand.
7. User reaches the dashboard.

### 11.2 Create Product and Generate Content

1. User selects a brand.
2. User creates a product.
3. User adds product data and assets.
4. User starts the product-to-content workflow.
5. AI generates listing and content drafts.
6. Quality checks run.
7. User reviews outputs.
8. User approves selected outputs.
9. System stores the approved versions.
10. User publishes later through a connector.

### 11.3 Connect a Marketplace

1. User opens Integrations.
2. User selects a marketplace connector.
3. System shows supported capabilities and required permissions.
4. User completes authentication.
5. System encrypts credentials.
6. Connector health check runs.
7. User maps the marketplace account to a brand.
8. System displays synchronization readiness.

### 11.4 Publish Approved Content

1. User opens approved content.
2. User selects a connected channel.
3. System validates required fields and assets.
4. System shows the final publishing payload.
5. User approves publishing.
6. Connector publishes.
7. System records external identifiers and URL.
8. Analytics collection is scheduled where supported.

### 11.5 Recover Failed Workflow

1. User receives a workflow failure notification.
2. User opens the execution details.
3. System highlights the failed step and reason.
4. User corrects configuration or input.
5. User retries the failed step.
6. Workflow resumes without duplicating completed external actions.

## 14. Acceptance Criteria for the MVP

The MVP will be accepted when all of the following are true:

1. The application installs and launches on a supported Windows machine.
2. A user can create and authenticate with an owner account.
3. A user can create a brand and product.
4. A user can securely configure an AI provider.
5. A user can execute an AI-assisted product workflow.
6. The workflow can pause for approval.
7. A user can approve or reject generated outputs.
8. Execution state survives an application restart.
9. One marketplace connector demonstrates successful authentication and at least one supported data operation.
10. One publishing connector demonstrates successful authenticated publishing or a complete sandbox-equivalent flow.
11. Secrets are encrypted and redacted.
12. Backups can be created and restored.
13. Critical actions appear in audit history.
14. Core modules have automated tests.
15. Known critical and high-severity defects are resolved.
16. Architecture, setup, user, and troubleshooting documentation exists.

## 18. Risks and Mitigations

### Risk: Overly Broad Scope

**Mitigation:** Enforce milestone boundaries, vertical slices, and explicit non-goals.

### Risk: Integration Access Delays

**Mitigation:** Build mock connectors, sandbox adapters, and capability-driven contracts.

### Risk: AI Output Quality

**Mitigation:** Human approval, evaluation, structured output, versioned prompts, and quality gates.

### Risk: External API Changes

**Mitigation:** Versioned connector interfaces and contract tests.

### Risk: Credential Exposure

**Mitigation:** Encrypted secret storage, redaction, least privilege, and rotation.

### Risk: Workflow Side Effects

**Mitigation:** Idempotency, approval, dry-run mode, and execution audit.

### Risk: High AI Cost

**Mitigation:** Local models, budgets, caching, model routing, and usage dashboards.

### Risk: Developer Burnout

**Mitigation:** Small sprints, clear completion criteria, and working releases.

### Risk: Architecture Overengineering

**Mitigation:** Modular monolith, practical abstractions, and ADR-based decisions.
